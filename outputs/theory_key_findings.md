# R-generated key findings

All statistics below were generated from `R/11_theory_associational_models.R`.
They describe archived congressional messages and supplementary adjusted party comparisons; they are not causal effects, validated stance labels, or misinformation judgments.

## Finding 1: attention converged while policy opposition diverged
- During Omicron, Democratic and Republican accounts posted 54.40 and 53.56 COVID tweets per 1,000 archived tweets, respectively.
- Yet the selected mandate-conflict terms appeared in 0.50% of Democratic and 15.84% of Republican COVID candidates.
- The adjusted Republican-to-Democratic odds ratio was 9.43 (95% CI 7.24-12.28) during Delta and 38.04 (24.96-57.95) during Omicron.

## Finding 2: narrow skepticism language was highly concentrated
- The top 10% of accounts produced 85.03% of the 648 narrow skepticism-marker tweets; the effective number of equally productive accounts was 15.70.

## Finding 3: mandate-conflict matches were also concentrated, but the party pattern survives an outlier check
- The top 10% of accounts produced 47.29% of all mandate-conflict dictionary matches.
- After removing the ten most prolific mandate-opposition accounts, Republican versus Democratic shares remained 14.78% versus 1.92% during Delta and 15.17% versus 0.50% during Omicron.

- The stance-and-frame annotation sample is prepared, but no human-coded stance or frame results should be reported until two independent coders complete the instrument and reliability is evaluated.
